Giving a factory owner real-time control of production

A garment export manufacturer couldn't see where its orders really stood until someone reconciled a spreadsheet by hand. I worked with the owner to understand what the business needed to know each day. Then I used AI to build an app that gives him that answer live, at no running cost.

ClientGarment export manufacturer
Who benefitsOwner, merchandiser and floor leads
How it was builtAI-assisted development
Running cost$0 / month

The business problem

The owner is responsible for delivering export orders to international buyers on time and in full. Production status lived in a shared Excel file, which left him with five problems:

  • No live view. To find out where an order stood, someone had to make calls or reconcile the sheet.
  • Losses found too late. Pieces lost or rejected between stages often surfaced only near shipment, when it was hardest to recover.
  • Outsourced work out of sight. Goods sent to outside dyeing or ironing units were hard to follow up on.
  • Numbers couldn't be trusted. Anyone could overwrite a figure, and there was no record of who changed it.
  • Packing details pieced together by hand. Carton-by-carton contents for the buyer had to be assembled manually.

Together, these put delivery dates, margins and buyer confidence at risk.

What changes for the owner and the business

One live view of every order

Output today, this week and in total for each PO and each stage, in one place. The owner sees status without having to chase anyone.

Losses caught where they happen

Rejections and shortfalls show up at the stage they occur. The team can act while there's still time to recover the quantity.

Control over outsourced work

Every batch sent to an outside unit is tracked: who has it, when it went out, and how much came back good, rejected or short.

Numbers the owner can trust

Each person enters only their own stage. Only the owner can correct a figure, and every correction is recorded.

Buyer-ready packing information

Numbered cartons record exactly which styles and sizes are inside, so packing lists don't have to be rebuilt by hand.

Fits how the business already works

Everything exports to Excel, which the team already uses. Hosting costs nothing, so there's no new overhead.

How I delivered it

I treated the owner and the CEO as my customers and ran the project like a customer success engagement.

  • Started from the business questions. Where is each order? Where are we losing pieces? What's still with vendors? Those answers shaped everything that followed.
  • Decisions in plain language. I shared short documents with tick-box options and a recommended choice for each, so the owner could decide without any technical background.
  • Prototype first, then fast iteration. Leadership approved a prototype first. After the demo, their feedback, including new stages, outsourcing and tighter edit rights, went into the app within days.
  • Scope set by value. We focused on the production floor, where the pain was greatest, and parked the yarn-to-fabric module for later.
Built using AI. I'm not a full-time software developer. I used AI (Claude) as my development partner to write and refine the code. My own work went into the parts that decide whether it succeeds: understanding the business, defining the rules and data, testing against real scenarios, and getting stakeholders to sign off. This let a customer-facing professional deliver a working business application quickly and at no cost.

Where it stands

The app has been demonstrated to the owner and the CEO, and all the changes they asked for are done. It's now in client testing ahead of going live.

What this shows about how I work

  • I start with the outcome the customer needs, not with the tool.
  • I use data and AI to close the gap between a business problem and a working solution.
  • I keep non-technical stakeholders confident by giving them clear choices and quick turnarounds.
