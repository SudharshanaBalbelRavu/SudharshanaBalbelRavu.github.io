From a status spreadsheet to a production app

A garment export manufacturer was tracking every order through a shared Excel status file. I built a web app around each person's role to replace it. It covers every stage from cutting to packed cartons, including work sent to outside vendors.

My roleRequirements, design, build, delivery
Users5 roles, ~8 people
StackNext.js · Supabase
Running cost$0 / month

The problem

Production for each purchase order (PO) passes through up to seven stages, and the order differs from style to style. The team tracked it all in one Excel status file. That caused four recurring problems:

  • Losses showed up late. Pieces lost between stages only came to light when someone reconciled the sheet by hand.
  • Cartons couldn't be tracked properly. A single carton often holds several styles and sizes, and a flat sheet can't record that.
  • Outside work was invisible. Nobody could easily see what had gone to outside dyeing or ironing units, when it went, or how much came back good.
  • Anyone could overwrite a number. There was no record of who changed what.

I started by building a stronger Excel workbook, with separate planner and floor-manager views, SUMIFS dashboards and flags for stage-to-stage losses. It helped, but the business needed proper multi-user access and real control over who can enter and edit what. That needed an app.

How I ran it

I ran the project the way I'd run a customer engagement. The owner and the CEO were the customer, and nothing got built until they had signed off on it.

  • Discovery. I took the stage logic and the wastage rules from the existing status file and checked them against how the floor actually works.
  • Decision documents. I wrote requirements in plain language with tick-box options, recommended a choice for each, and listed open questions, so the owner could decide without any technical background.
  • Prototype first. The owner approved a clickable prototype, and then I built the real app step by step.
  • Short feedback loops. After the leadership demo, I shipped the requested changes as database migrations within days. These included renaming a stage, adding Tie-Dye, restricting who can see packing, adding Excel export on every tab, and later supporting in-house units and outsourcing.
The scope changed partway through. The original plan covered the full chain from yarn to fabric. Once it was clear the biggest pain was on the production floor, we narrowed it to cutting through packing. The yarn-to-fabric module is parked for a later phase.

What the app does

Each PO works like a project. It starts as a draft, goes active, and is closed when the order is done. Inside a PO are styles, and inside each style are variants for each combination of gender, colour, size and print design.

Daily entry grid

Each stage lead enters the day's numbers in a spreadsheet-style grid, filtered by buyer and PO. Running totals and the balance still to do are calculated automatically, never typed in by hand.

Mixed-style cartons

Cartons are numbered within each PO and can hold several styles and sizes. Style details carry over from the previous stage and can be edited. A carton can be changed while it's open and is final once sealed. Only the owner can reopen it.

Outsourcing

Work sent out records the vendor (picked from a saved list), the date sent and the date returned. Returns are logged as good, rejected or short, so outside work is tracked just like in-house production.

Analytics & export

Shows output for today, this week and in total for each PO, with filters, alongside a status board. Every tab exports to Excel, because that's the format the business already works in.

Roles and controls

RoleCan doCan't do
OwnerSee and edit everything, correct saved numbers, delete wrong entries, close or reopen POsNothing is off limits, and every change is logged
MerchandiserCreate POs, styles and variants, record outsourcing, see everythingEdit or delete saved data
Cutting leadEnter cutting figuresSee other stages' entry screens, edit or delete
Production leadEnter sewing, printing/embroidery, checking and ironing figuresEdit or delete; see packing
Packing leadFill and seal cartonsEdit or delete sealed data

These rules are enforced in the database itself through Postgres Row-Level Security, not just hidden in the interface. Closing a PO before its full quantity is produced requires a reason. Corrections can only be made by the owner, and each one goes into an audit log. Every line item has its own comment thread, so questions stay attached to the data they're about.

Where it stands

The full cutting-to-packing workflow is built and has been demonstrated to the owner and the CEO. The rounds of changes requested after the demo are done, including units, outsourcing and tighter edit rights. The app is now in client testing, and deployment to Cloudflare comes next.

What I took away

  • Plain-language decision documents got faster and clearer answers from a non-technical owner than any technical spec would have.
  • Putting the rules in the database, through row-level security, generated columns and audit logs, made the app trustworthy. Hiding buttons in the interface wouldn't have.
  • Scoping down to the floor's biggest pain point delivered value sooner than building the whole chain first.
